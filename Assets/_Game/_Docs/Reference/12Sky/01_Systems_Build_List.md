## Glossary: Twelve Sky reference → Krytical Nova term

Every system, item, place and stat in this doc has one Krytical Nova name, chosen to fit the setting (Hegemony corporate tech, Null machines, Covenant clans, comic-book register) rather than copied. The reference column holds the Twelve Sky 2 term and, in brackets, the 12Sky (first game) term from the help pages where it differs. "In code" names what the repo calls it today; a different display name costs one string, a different identifier one rename. "What it is for" says in a line or two what the thing does for a player, and whether it is built.

## Variance notes

For each item on the owner's list, how the Krytical Nova version differs from its Twelve Sky reference and what is worth taking or leaving.

**Saving.** Twelve Sky never saves on the client. The repo is already shaped the same way: `ProgressionState` is server-held and resent on change, and `ICharacterStore` abstracts where characters live (PlayerPrefs today, a server store later). What is missing is the server side of that seam: persist progression, Pack, equipment and position per character. Keep one serializable state object per character so a save is one write.

**Stats (TS2: Character Stats Distribution).** The Phase 1 sheet already has everything Twelve Sky shows and more (Crit Damage, Lockup Resist, Move Speed, Luck). What is left is on the gear side: the per-slot base stats and the secondary ladder in Gear spec section 3, plus seven new stats (Attack Speed, Core Resist, Knockback Resist, Critical Defense, Kill-XP rate and amount, skill-line modifiers).

**Tempering** (TS2 Reinforcement, 12Sky Upgrade). The risky path in both games: percent success that climbs, −3% on a fail, a break chance that starts at 60%, a cap of 120% (150% is a later system). The Broker's NPC text already uses "12% or more" as the Exchange threshold, so the percent model is what the repo expects. Full rules in Gear spec section 4.

**Fusion** (TS2 Combination, 12Sky Combine). Identical in both references: 12 stages, Rare+ only, same level only, one clean material consumed. Two rules worth copying: a fail never destroys the target, and a fused item cannot be fusion material. Gear spec section 5.

**Tuning** (TS2 Refine). 12Sky has nothing like it; its closest pages are Item Exchange and Rank Up / Down. The owner's notes give the per-slot gains per stage to 25, which is the whole system; Gear spec section 6 fills in materials and odds. Slotting + Cores (section 7) and Epic Mk (section 8) round out the gear layer.

**Hostiles and AI (TS2: monsters, Field Champions).** Twelve Sky's rules are about level gap, not AI: grey name = no XP, pink = normal, red = dangerous, and drop chance falls off when you out-level a Hostile by 10+. Emerald AI handles aggro, wander and combat; you still need the level-gap tint on the HP bar and the XP / drop gate, which are game rules, not AI rules. Bruisers (field champions) are the mid-tier target with better drops.

**Autopilot with Magnet (TS2: none; nearest Battue hunt).** Twelve Sky has no auto hunt; it has the Sweep (pull up to 5 Hostiles, AoE them, more than 5 and they counter). Keep that 5-Hostile cap as a tunable so Autopilot does not pull the whole zone. Magnet has no reference either; pickup is a key or a click.

**Ascension and Nova Standings (TS2: CP Reinforcement halo, CP stat Titles).** Both have a direct Twelve Sky reference: CP Reinforcement (100 CP + 1,000,000 Silver per attempt, +1 to all four attributes per level, a new halo every 8 levels) and the CP stat Titles L1–L12 (800 to 10,000 CP). Both are built at the new Herald NPC (7 Oct 2026); the Nova Store stays real-money. Where ours differ: an attempt costs half the Creds, a slip never takes a Nova tier once it is reached, every Standings track held counts (not only the one worn), and the rank words are each faction's own. Gear spec section 9. The Bounty Board is our own; it is a Renown source, not a title mechanic.

**Combat skills for 8 weapons (TS2: weapon martial-art lines).** The Blade line already follows the Twelve Sky slot pattern (single, multi, AoE, war-god AoE, stance buff), so the other 8 lines are a `BladeSkillBuilder` clone per weapon with new names, animations and `SkillFeelCatalog` entries. The unlock cadence (Instructor for 1st–3rd, Skill Chip at 30 and 60, Dossier for the 8th) matches the `requiresSkillBook` flag and Archive gating; Phase 5 of design 10 is the plan for it.

**Trade and the Exchange (TS2: Trade, Consignment).** Same shape as the page 13 flow: Shift+click target, request, both parties add items and Creds, both Accept, items land in the Pack. The Exchange (consignment board at the Broker) is the asynchronous half: list a Rare at 12%+ Temper with a Commission Receipt, browse, buy.

**Picking up items (TS2: pickup key / click).** Twelve Sky binds pickup to a key and to left-click on the drop; that is the manual half of Magnet.

**Vault and Deep Vault (TS2: Storage, Warehouse).** Twelve Sky has two tiers and the original list named one. Per-character Vault at the Vaultkeeper in the Home City, and account-wide Deep Vault at the Wayfinder in every town. The two-tier split is the thing to copy: the account-wide tier is what makes alt characters useful.

**Kiosks (TS2: Private Shop).** Twelve Sky's private shops are player-manned: the seller is frozen while the shop is open. Proxy selling implies the Kiosk runs while the player is away or offline, which the reference does not do; the rest (shop name, price entry, Start, sale message, no take-back once sold, zone-restricted to the Central Plaza and Home Cities) transfers as-is. **Decided 5 Oct 2026:** proxy selling, running while the owner is offline, with the duration chosen at set-up (3 days free, 14 or 30 days with a Kiosk Permit from the Nova Store).

**Squad (TS2: Party).** Twelve Sky caps at 5 and requires members within 5 levels of each other, XP bonus +20 / 30 / 40 / 50% per extra member, same map only. The level-gap cap is the design choice to decide on; it stops power-leveling but kills mixed-level friend groups.

**Sponsor / Recruit (TS2: Mentor / Disciple).** Twelve Sky's Mentor: Sponsor must be Prime 1+, Recruit level 122 or below, Recruit gets 1.5x XP only when the Sponsor is in the same area, does not stack with Squad XP, Sponsor earns 250 points per minute while nearby. The non-stacking rule and the proximity requirement are the two levers that stop it being a free XP buff.

**Callouts (TS2: Duel).** Built (as `PlayerDuel`). Ours are to the death with forfeit, distance and disconnect rules; Twelve Sky's are 180 s with a knock-out win, a pre-fight Stim toggle, and arena-only. The Stim toggle and the hard timer are cheap additions if a non-lethal Callout mode is wanted.

**Outfitter, Stylist, Reshaper, Coach (TS2: Tailor; the rest are new).** Not in Twelve Sky (its Tailor only sells armor and crafts wings). The character creator already has the panels (body type, palette, expression, lineage), so these NPCs are mostly a way to reopen those panels in-world for Creds and write the result back through `CharacterRecipe`. Gear dye at the Outfitter is the only new piece.

**Settings (TS2: Game Option Setup).** Further than Twelve Sky already: the O-key Settings window has HUD toggles and a full drag / resize / scale layout editor that persists. The reference's ESC menu only picks the Hotbar mode. What is left is the three standard tabs: graphics (resolution, quality, vsync, the comic-style toggles from the Color Bible), audio (mixer sliders), and key rebinding through the Input System rebind flow so Hotbar keys, camera keys and the window keys all live in one list.

**Servers and Lines (TS2: servers and channels).** Twelve Sky 2 splits its world into channels and brings everyone together for wars. Ours began the same way and ended somewhere simpler (owner, 11 Oct 2026): **one open world**, one copy of every map and one server per map and per war. A copy is a **Line**, the owner's word, because "channel" already means chat here. A second Line exists only by rule: a map that measurement shows one server cannot hold, a war with more players queued than it holds, The Scar when enough wait for a fair fight. A war's queue is split into even wars rather than turning anyone away, and when the factions turn up unevenly the crowded side is offered places as **Mercs** on a short side for that one event: a carbon copy of the character in the other faction's look, so nobody has to sit out. Beacon Wars take no Mercs and are open to absolutely everyone; when more come than one server holds, another front opens (a front is one Line of each map on the war's road) and the one Beacon is shared by every front. Lines do not save money (each adds its own memory and Hostiles); they buy room. It stays one server program per map, not one for the whole game, because a Unity server works on one processor core. Planetary Processing was looked at for hosting and dropped: its server is Lua scripts on its own machines, with no Unity, so every server-side system would be rewritten, and its free plan stops at 10 players.

**The Scar (TS2: Yanggok Valley).** In Twelve Sky 2 the valley sits in the middle of all the faction lands and is open from level 90. It is permanent PvP between factions, it holds the portals to each faction's high dungeons, and it is the road a Holy Stone Battle is fought along: the attackers start there and break guardian shields map by map until they reach the stone (the guides read put it on the defender's second map; the owner, who played it, puts the stone in the home city map, which the other factions can attack, and that is what ours follows: the Beacon stands in the Home City). Taking a stone raised the taker's damage and lowered the loser's (the guides disagree on 10 % or 20 %) and battles ran for three hours on set evenings. What to take: one shared warzone that every faction has to cross, with no Lines. What is ours to decide: the bonus, the schedule, and what the Ronin's maps add to it.

**Ronin (TS2: the fourth faction, Nangin).** Twelve Sky 2 opens a fourth faction to characters at Master 1, drawn from the other three. Joining ends guild membership, clears the friend list and cannot be undone. Ours (owner, 11 Oct 2026): rogue players from the three factions; only the look of their gear and their HUD change, and their weapon class and skills stay. That keeps it to content (a skin, a HUD kit, a gear look) with no new classes. Decided 15 Oct 2026 (owner): entry from Prime level 1, Crew and Contacts are left behind as in Twelve Sky 2, it is permanent, and the Ronin have a Home City (its name is in the owner's Figma).

**Crew Halls (no TS2 equivalent).** Twelve Sky has no housing; the references are other games. Final Fantasy XIV sells a fixed number of plots in wards by lottery and demolishes a house left alone for 45 days: the shortage and the demolition are its most disliked rules. World of Warcraft (Midnight) opens new neighbourhood copies as they fill, charges no upkeep, and has a Basic and an Advanced decorating mode. The Elder Scrolls Online has undo and linked pieces but no copy tool, which its players add themselves. The Sims is the standard for comfort: grid steps, free placing on a key, an eyedropper, walls that drop out of the way. What to take: copies of one neighbourhood with a set number of Lots, a new Line when all are full, no upkeep, two decorating modes, a visible budget, storage apart from the Pack. What is ours (owner, 10 Oct 2026): a Crew Fund pays, a piece stays its placer's and goes back to them, a quiet Crew's Hall is packed away and its Lot is paid for again, a Quarter is for its own faction, and so is the Facade of every Hall in it; what a Hall is furnished with, indoors and on its lawns, is anyone's (owner, 12 Oct 2026, replacing the rule of 11 Oct that a faction's furnishings were its own).
