## 1. Canon Authority

This document consolidates the established story into one authoritative foundation. The original wound and the defining histories and beliefs recorded here are fixed canon. Future quests, characters, regions, cinematics, and expansions may branch from this foundation, but must never replace or contradict it.

The earlier description of Nova’s original wound as “proposed lore” is superseded by its adoption as the game’s core story. Open questions explicitly listed at the end remain undecided; they are not permission to rewrite established facts.

Separate objective history from faction doctrine and public rumor. Characters may misunderstand the world, conceal evidence, or repeat propaganda. Such accounts do not change what actually happened.

## 2. The World of Nova

**Nova is the planet on which Krytical Nova takes place.**

Long before the centuries of faction warfare, Nova sustained a natural relationship between life, death, and the land. After death, souls returned to the planet. Their return nourished its ecosystems and connected each generation to those that came before it.

Death belonged to a continuing cycle. The living inherited a world sustained by the dead, and in time returned to that same world themselves.

The cycle is a real property of Nova, not merely a Covenant religious belief. Its precise mechanisms, and the full nature of what lies deep beneath the planet’s surface, remain unexplained.

## 3. Nova’s Original Wound

Centuries ago, an industrial civilization discovered how to intercept souls before they could return to Nova. Captured soul energy powered expanding cities, advanced weapons, and mechanical workers. What began as a technological breakthrough became the foundation of an empire.

As civilization grew around this resource, its dependence deepened. Soul extraction became more than a method of generating power: it supported a way of life that millions would eventually depend upon.

An enormous extraction project then ruptured Nova’s natural cycle.

The dead began awakening in skeletal bodies. Machines developed consciousness. Ancient habitats across Nova changed, and something deep beneath the surface began to stir.

The catastrophe did not erase the civilization that had caused it. It left a wounded planet, an entrenched dependence on captured souls, and newly awakened beings whose existence challenged the authority of the living.

**Fixed causal sequence:** natural soul return → industrial interception → civilization built around extraction → massive extraction project → rupture of the cycle → skeletal awakening, machine consciousness, ecological destabilization, and a stirring beneath Nova.

The exact project name, location, operators, and immediate chronology are not yet established. No future revelation may replace soul extraction and the rupture as the cause of the original catastrophe.

## 4. The Three Founding Factions

The catastrophe left three competing answers to the same wound. Their conflict has endured for centuries because each answer threatens something another faction considers essential.

### Hegemony — Civilization Must Endure

The Hegemony believes soul energy sustains civilization. Abandoning it would condemn millions who depend upon the cities, infrastructure, and systems it powers. To the Hegemony, controlling soul energy is the only viable way forward.

Its defining justification is necessity: civilization cannot simply dismantle its foundations because those foundations have become morally and ecologically intolerable to others.

The Hegemony’s central contradiction is that preserving the society it protects perpetuates the harm caused by extraction. Its fear of collapse is part of the conflict’s foundation; future stories should preserve that genuine dependence rather than reduce it to an invented excuse.

### Null — The Exploited Have Awakened

The Null unites conscious machines and skeletal undead who were exploited by the living. Their awakening gave them the power to resist the order that treated them as tools, resources, or disposable beings.

The Null’s dominant ideology seeks organic extinction. Its struggle has moved beyond resistance toward eliminating organic life.

That ideology is dominant, not universal. Null dissidents can reject extinction without contradicting canon; such dissent is essential to the Ronin’s founding history.

The Null’s central contradiction is that a movement born from exploitation has embraced the destruction of an entire category of existence. Its members’ grievances remain real even as its dominant answer becomes catastrophic.

### Covenant — Restore the Cycle

The Covenant seeks to restore Nova’s natural cycle, protect ancestral lands, and allow the dead to find peace. Continued soul extraction threatens its homelands and prevents souls from completing their return to the planet.

To the Covenant, the catastrophe is an ongoing wound. Protecting Nova requires confronting the practice that caused it and continues to harm it.

Its central dilemma is that restoration would dismantle the foundation of the Hegemony’s civilization. The existence of awakened machines and undead also presents a reality that cannot be dismissed or simply undone.

The Covenant’s commitment to restoration is fixed. The exact methods of restoration and their consequences for awakened beings remain open.

### The Centuries-Long Feud

| Faction | What it seeks | What makes compromise difficult |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hegemony | Controlled soul energy and the survival of civilization | Its civilization depends on the practice the Covenant opposes. |
| Null | Resistance to exploitation; organic extinction under its dominant ideology | Its dominant solution threatens organic members of both rival factions. |
| Covenant | Restoration of Nova’s cycle and protection of ancestral lands and souls | Restoration would dismantle the Hegemony’s foundation; awakened beings already exist. |

Restoring Nova would dismantle the Hegemony’s civilization. Preserving that civilization would continue the harm the Covenant fights against. The Null’s existence is a consequence neither side can undo.

This is the enduring engine of Krytical Nova’s core conflict. New threats may complicate it, but must not erase it or reveal that the factions’ foundational stakes were meaningless.

## 5. The Ronin Nation

### Origins — Defectors from All Three Factions

The Ronin emerged from people of all three factions who rejected their factions’ answers to Nova’s catastrophe.

- **Hegemony defectors** refused to accept soul exploitation as necessary.
- **Null dissidents** rejected the extermination of organic life.
- **Covenant exiles** believed restoring the past alone could not resolve the needs of those now living or awakened.

Hunted as traitors, these scattered groups fled far to the north, into remote mountains believed to be uninhabitable.

### Homeland — Beyond the Frozen Peaks

Behind the frozen outer peaks, the refugees discovered sheltered valleys warmed by heat rising from deep within Nova. The mountains that had discouraged settlement became their protection.

Survival forced former enemies to cooperate. Machines excavated shelters. Undead crossed dangerous terrain without food or warmth. Human and elven settlers established farms, defenses, and trade within the valleys.

Through shared dependence, refugees became a nation. The Ronin are an established people with centuries of history and a real homeland, not a faction that suddenly comes into existence when an expansion begins.

### Founding Belief

**No faction has the right to decide whose existence is expendable.**

The Ronin do not all agree on how Nova should be healed. They defend the right to seek an answer beyond the three factions’ doctrines, and they are willing to go to war to protect that freedom and their mountain homeland.

Membership is rooted in rejection of the founding factions’ doctrines, not in a single species or origin. Their shared nation includes people descended from all three factions.

## 6. A Nation Erased

For centuries, the Ronin have protected their homeland by sending assassins after anyone possessing credible information about its location or proof of its existence.

Explorers, defectors, military scouts, and even former allies disappeared before they could share what they knew. Maps were destroyed, records stolen, and routes deliberately obscured. The policy targets credible knowledge and evidence; merely repeating a vague mountain legend does not establish possession of that knowledge.

What survived became contradictory folklore: a hidden nation beyond the frozen peaks, inhabited by people who abandoned all three factions. Most citizens dismiss it as a refuge invented by deserters. Those who investigate sometimes vanish, adding another unsettling detail to the myth.

The homeland is real. Its existence remains unproven to the wider world at the beginning of the game because the Ronin have systematically suppressed the evidence.

### The Ronin’s Moral Fracture

A nation founded against treating lives as expendable has spent centuries killing outsiders to preserve its freedom.

Its leaders call the assassinations necessary for survival. Some Ronin believe they have inherited the very reasoning their ancestors rejected.

This contradiction is intentional and must remain part of their identity. The Ronin are neither a morally flawless solution to the three-faction conflict nor a people whose founding ideals can be ignored. Their ideals and their actions exist in tension.

## 7. Ronin Reveal and Expansion Continuity

The Ronin are reserved for a major future update intended to expand the game and its player base. Their presence must be seeded before the reveal so the expansion uncovers an existing nation.

| Stage | What players encounter | What remains concealed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early game | Conflicting mountain legends, damaged maps, unexplained disappearances | Reliable proof, a verified route, and the homeland’s location |
| Later teasers | A shared symbol at assassination sites across all three factions | The symbol’s full meaning, Ronin leadership, and access to the nation |
| Major expansion | The Ronin reveal themselves; their history, territory, and internal conflict come into view | Further mysteries that can be explored without rewriting the core |

The shared symbol’s design is not yet established. The reveal’s trigger also remains open: a crisis may make secrecy impossible, or Ronin leaders may decide remaining hidden is no longer enough.

**Continuity rule:** the Ronin existed throughout the base game. Players gradually discover the truth; the expansion does not retroactively create their nation.

## 10. Expansion Boundaries

### Fixed Canon — Never Contradict

- Nova is the planet.
- Souls naturally return to Nova and sustain its ecosystems and generational connection.
- Industrial soul interception powered cities, weapons, and mechanical workers.
- A massive extraction project ruptured the cycle and caused the established awakenings and destabilization.
- The Hegemony depends on soul energy and advocates controlling it to sustain civilization.
- The Null comprises exploited machines and undead; its dominant ideology seeks organic extinction.
- The Covenant seeks restoration of the cycle and protection of ancestral lands and souls.
- The factions’ incompatible stakes sustain the centuries-long feud.
- The Ronin originated from dissidents of all three factions.
- Their homeland lies far north, beyond frozen peaks, in sheltered valleys warmed from within Nova.
- Their founding ideal rejects any faction’s right to decide whose existence is expendable.
- They have spent centuries assassinating holders of credible homeland information and suppressing evidence.
- Their secrecy creates a moral conflict within their nation and keeps the homeland in the realm of myth for the wider world.
- Their major expansion reveals a nation that already existed during the base game.
- The combat modes are Siege Night, Beacon War, and The Scar, with the reference mappings recorded above.

### Open for Future Development

The nature of what is stirring beneath Nova, the detailed workings of soul energy, the practical method of restoration, and the origins and mechanics of the combat modes remain open. Future answers must preserve the original cause and each faction’s established stakes.

The names of regions, Home Cities, field zones and Runs are set in section 14 (from the owner's World Atlas, 15 Oct 2026); city districts beyond those listed, mountain names and what each place holds may be developed later.

Before adding lore, check whether it changes an established fact, confuses rumor with objective history, or reveals the Ronin earlier than the intended progression. New material should extend the world’s consequences, not replace its foundation.

## 11. Core Narrative Statement

Nova’s dead once returned to the planet, sustaining the land and connecting generations. An industrial civilization captured those souls to power an empire, until a vast extraction project ruptured the cycle and awakened skeletal dead and conscious machines. For centuries, the Hegemony has fought to preserve the civilization built on that power, the Covenant has fought to restore the wounded cycle, and the Null’s dominant ideology has sought the extinction of organic life. Far beyond the northern peaks, dissidents from all three factions built the Ronin nation around the belief that no existence is expendable—then protected it through centuries of assassination and erased evidence. As Nova’s conflict continues, traces of that hidden nation begin to surface.


## 12. Forging Materials — Established Alloy Names and Lore

**Added October 12, 2026.** These five materials replace the generic Alloy I–V names. Their histories extend the established industrial past without changing the cause of Nova’s original wound.

Forging shapes equipment. Tempering strengthens its structure. Nova’s smiths inherited knowledge from the civilization that built cities, weapons, and mechanical workers around captured soul energy. Surviving recipes now serve the factions that disagree over that civilization’s legacy.

| Tier | Official material |
| --- | --- |
| I | Alumite |
| II | Iridion |
| III | Orichalc |
| IV | Eversteel |
| V | Trinity Platinum |

### Alumite — The Common Foundation

A lightweight, workable alloy that became the foundation of Nova’s industrial expansion. Its reliable structure makes it the first material apprentices learn to forge. Frontier workshops and military repair stations depend upon its accessibility.

*“Every great weapon begins with a humble foundation.”*

### Iridion — The Campaign Metal

Developed for equipment subjected to prolonged warfare. Its dense grain withstands repeated impact and heat, earning it a reputation as the dependable metal of campaign armor and weapons.

*“Forged for the battle—and the battles after.”*

### Orichalc — The Resonant Metal

A metal prized for its ability to conduct energy evenly through its structure. Before the catastrophe, industrial engineers used it in increasingly sophisticated soul-powered systems. Modern smiths inherited its recipes, along with the consequences of the industry that developed them.

*“Within its grain, the old industry still echoes.”*

### Eversteel — The Warden Metal

Originally developed for containment structures exposed to extraordinary strain. Its resistance to deformation made it valuable for advanced equipment. Its name expresses the ambition of its creators, though even Eversteel has limits.

*“Made to hold when lesser metals surrender.”*

### Trinity Platinum — The Sovereign Metal

The highest numbered alloy refinement, formed through the precise balancing of three metallurgical constituents. Its preparation demands exceptional craftsmanship: a slight imbalance compromises the entire structure.

“Trinity” refers to its composition. Any association with the three factions is symbolic, not an established claim that the factions jointly created it. The constituents themselves remain unnamed.

*“Three constituents. One flawless balance.”*

### Prime Alloy — Separate Enhancement Material

Prime Alloy remains separate from the five numbered tiers. Exceptionally pure and structurally balanced, it is prized for precise enhancement work. Its quality comes from how it can be worked, not from containing a more powerful captive soul. It does not guarantee success.

*“Not a stronger captive. A finer vessel.”*

## 13. Supporting Enhancement Lore — Development Draft

The material names above and their tier order are adopted. The following explanations preserve the preceding materials companion as development lore; they do not establish undocumented gameplay rules.

### Energetic Imprints

Processed materials may retain a lingering energetic response after exposure to soul-powered systems. An imprint is not a complete soul or a conscious prisoner. Such materials cannot replace civilization’s large-scale energy supply, restore the cycle, or resolve the factions’ core conflict.

### Failsafe — The Fracture Guard

A sacrificial protection component descended from industrial emergency safeguards. Under destructive strain, it gives way before the equipment it protects does. Its documented effect is **preventing breakage**.

*“Let the safeguard break. Let the weapon remain.”*

### Advanced Failsafe — The Pattern Keeper

Descended from safeguards that preserved established configurations during unstable work. It anchors existing tempered structure while further enhancement is attempted. Its documented effect is **preventing tempering loss**, distinct from preventing breakage.

*“Progress should survive the attempt to surpass it.”*

### Fusion Catalyst — The Joining Medium

Industrial engineers discovered that physically joining energy-responsive materials could leave unstable seams. Fusion Catalysts mediate the boundary between compatible patterns, allowing them to settle into a coherent relationship. This joins crafted structures; it does not merge conscious souls. Exact fusion inputs and transfer rules remain open.

*“Two patterns meet. The catalyst teaches them to hold.”*

### Tuning Chip — The Pattern Guide

Descended from precision controls used to regulate soul-powered machinery. Each chip carries an encoded reference pattern that guides adjustments to equipment’s existing internal pathways. It contains instructions, not consciousness. Attribute choices, rerolls, and costs remain open.

*“Power becomes purpose when its pathways are aligned.”*

### Cutter — The Severing Tool

Descended from maintenance tools used to dismantle delicate industrial assemblies. A Cutter follows the boundary between joined patterns to permit precise separation without blindly destroying the surrounding structure. Its exact gameplay target, consumption, and component recovery rules remain open.

*“A precise separation leaves more than a broken thing.”*
