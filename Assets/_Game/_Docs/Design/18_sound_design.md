# The Sound Bible: how Krytical Nova sounds

Written 20 Oct 2026 from the music and sound plan the owner approved that day. Row 67 of the build list
(`../Reference/12Sky/01_Systems_Build_List.md`). Cadence, the fight beat and the one music mechanic built so far, is its
own page: `17_music_and_cadence.md`. Story canon is the `Lore-Bible.md`; place names come from `World-Atlas.md`.

**Status: planned.** Nothing on this page is in the game yet, except what section 3 names as already built (the fight
music of Cadence, the mixer, the weather loops). Numbers marked *(my pick)* have not been reviewed.

**The owner's rules (20 Oct 2026):**

- **Instrumental only.** No voices, chants, shouts or choirs anywhere in the music. A Run called the Bone Choir gets
  bowed metal and organ-like pads, not singing. Spoken lines and crowd murmur are ambience or NPC voices, never part of
  a music cue.
- **The lean is Hi-Fi Rush:** guitar-driven rhythm rock over big-beat electronics, and a world that moves on the beat.
- **The main inspiration for all the music is RJ Pasin and Dante Swan** (owner, 20 Oct 2026): instrumental guitar
  producers who fuse metal guitar with trap production. Section 1, "The sound we aim at".
- **No jazz** (owner, 20 Oct 2026, from the Volume 1 picks): no swing, brushed drums, Rhodes, lounge chords or
  "toonish city" lo-fi anywhere. Quiet cues stay in the same heavy, epic world as the title, just lower.
- **How it is made:** sketches with ACE-Step 1.5 XL on this PC, finals with Google Flow Music (driven through Claude in
  Chrome). Section 7.
- **Where it lives:** every music file in `G:\My Drive\KryticalNova\Audio\Music\KN-Originals`.
- **Nothing goes in the game until the owner approves it on an audition page.**

## 1. Sound soul: Soul-Engine Rock

On Nova souls are fuel. Cities, weapons and mechanical workers ran on captured souls, and still do (Lore Bible 3). So
the music is the engine: a groove you feel through the floor, like a machine room two decks down. Every faction plays
it, each in its own instruments, because every faction lives in the world that fuel made.

As in Hi-Fi Rush, you fight *to* the music. That is not decoration: Cadence judges a skill press within 90 ms of the
beat (`17_music_and_cadence.md`, section 4), so every fight track needs a **clear kick and snare** a player can lock to
without thinking. Busy fills, swung grooves and tempo drift are out of fight music; they are welcome in a Home City.

### The sound we aim at: RJ Pasin and Dante Swan

The owner's main inspiration for all the music (20 Oct 2026). Both are instrumental guitarist-producers:

- **RJ Pasin:** virtuoso guitar fusing metalcore and deathcore with hyperpop and trap; anime and video-game minded
  titles ("Isekai", "Final Battle", "Heroes 2.0"); heavy, saturated sound design.
- **Dante Swan:** djent fused with trap and electronics ("Eyeless", "Arcane", "Soul Snatcher"); has played with RJ Pasin.

What Krytical Nova takes from them, and what every prompt asks for:

- **Extended-range guitar** (7 and 8 string): tight, palm-muted djent chugs locked to the kick, then soaring tapped or
  legato leads for the hook. The riff is the identity, as in Doom: recognisable in one bar.
- **Trap production under the metal:** 808 sub-bass that follows the riff, rolling hi-hats, hard claps and snares on
  the backbeat, half-time drops. This is what makes it feel modern rather than plain metal.
- **Electronic sound design:** glitch stutters, risers, reversed swells, saturated synth layers between guitar phrases.
- **Epic, anime-boss energy:** big, heroic and fun, never gloomy or lounge-like.
- **Kept from Hi-Fi Rush:** a steady, clear beat (Cadence), a punchy rock drive, a world that moves on it.
- **Left out:** their vocals (instrumental only) and Dante Swan's nu-jazz side (no jazz).

Volume 1's picks (20 Oct 2026) confirm the direction: the Flow fight track "Battle Rhythm" (punk rock over big beat) is
the vibe for fights; the industrial Null coat and the war-drum Covenant coat were kept; the funk-jazz Selection theme,
the funk Hegemony coat and the lo-fi Concord bed were dropped as "toonish", "city-scape" and "jazz".

### The Broken Return motif

Four rising notes where the fourth never lands. Scale steps 1, 3, 5 and then the octave, the home note: the return.
The first three are played plainly; the fourth is cut off by a drum hit or swallowed by a distorted chord before it
sounds, the way the extraction broke the cycle and the dead stopped coming home (Lore Bible 3).

- It runs through the title, the menus, the stingers and every faction theme, in each faction's instruments: Hegemony
  synth brass, Null a drop-tuned riff, Covenant the hardanger fiddle.
- What cuts the fourth note says who is playing. The Hegemony cuts it clean (a gated snare, a tidy stop). The Null cut
  it with a bitcrushed hit. The Covenant let it ring into a war drum, almost landing.
- **The full resolution, the fourth note landing, is held back.** No cue lets it land. It is saved for a story moment
  about the cycle's return, if the story ever reaches one; how or whether restoration happens is open canon (Lore
  Bible 10), so the owner decides when, and nothing before that may spend it.

selection screen swell from one coat to another without a cut, and what makes The Scar possible.

### Tempo and key map

| Where | Tempo | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menus and the front end | 92 to 100 beats a minute | Half-time: the groove breathes |
| Exploring a place | 84 to 110 | Walking pace, by the place's mood |
| A fight with Hostiles | 140 to 150 | Driving rock |
| Callouts | 150 to 160 | Tighter and faster: one against one |
| War (Siege Night, Beacon War, The Scar) | 165 to 174 | A drum-and-bass feel |

**What Cadence keeps time to.** A track's `MusicTrack` beat is the beat a player would stomp to. For a war track in a
drum-and-bass feel that is the half-time pulse (82 to 87 beats a minute), not every sixteenth of the drums.

*Open for the owner:* the window is fixed at 90 ms either side. At 120 beats a minute a random press lands in it about
one time in three; at 150 about one in two. Either that is fine (fights feel generous), or the window shrinks with the
tempo so it stays about a third of a beat, which would be a new number in `CadenceSettings`. Nothing changes until the
owner picks.

**One key family per place,** so a bed, its fight tracks and its stingers can cross without clashing. All *(my picks)*:

| Region | Home key | Places |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 2. What makes combat music slap

What other games do, and what we take. From memory, not checked against sources on the day.

| Game | What it does | What we take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hi-Fi Rush | The world moves on the beat; every action lands on it. | A kick and snare clear enough to fight to; the world pulsing with the beat in a fight (Cadence M6). |
| Devil May Cry 5 | Layers join the track as the style rank climbs. | Vertical layers: the fight grows as it heats up, and thins as it cools. |
| Doom (2016) | A one-bar riff you remember after one fight. | Every fight track is built round one riff of a bar or two, stated in the first eight bars. |
| Metal Gear Rising | The vocal payoff is saved for the climax. | The payoff is saved, though ours is never a vocal: the Peak section and the lead stem are held back for the moment that earns them. |

**How the music changes.** Two ways, and we use both:

- **Vertical layering:** one track in stems played together (drums, bass, harmony, lead), each faded in or out. For
  growing within a fight.
- **Horizontal resequencing:** jumping to another section or another track. For changing state (exploring to fighting,
  fighting to Peak). Every jump waits for a **bar line**, never mid-bar.
- **Stingers over the seams:** a short hit (a cymbal swell, a riff stab, the Broken Return cut short) covers the moment
  one piece hands over to the next, so a tempo jump never shows.
- **Silence earns the drop.** A bar or two of near silence (drums out, a filter closing) before the fight track lands
  makes it hit twice as hard. Used sparingly: on Peak, on a Callout's countdown, on a boss's entrance.
- **Big hits duck the music.** A heavy blow, a Critical, a Bruiser's slam or a Redline hit pulls the music down for a
  moment so the hit's comic sound effect cracks through. A Duck Volume on the Music group, fed by the
  impact sounds, in `KN_Main`.

## 3. Music states (planned)

```
 Front End ──► Explore (day / night) ──► Alert ──► Fight ──► Peak
                     ▲                     │         │  Skirmish · Callout · War · Boss
                     └──── cools down ─────┴─────────┘
```

| State | When | What plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Front End** | Title, faction select, creator, character select, loading | The front-end cues of section 4. |
| **Explore** | In the world, nothing after the player | The place's bed: a day version and a night version, swapped on the day cycle. Calm, with space in it. |
| **Alert** | A Hostile has turned on the player but no blow has been given or taken yet; or a player of an enemy faction is within 40 m *(my pick)* in a zone whose `ZonePvpRule` is `FactionWar` or `AlwaysOn`, checked with `FactionRules.AreHostile` | The fight track's drums and bass come in on the conductor's next bar; the Explore bed filters down under them. The beat is live, so Cadence works. |
| **Fight** | A blow given or taken, an attack skill cast, a Callout | Harmony joins; the lead joins when the fight heats up (a second enemy, or three Cadence presses in a row; music only, no bonus) *(my picks)* and leaves at the bar after it cools. |
| **Peak** | A boss's last phase, a Callout's last stretch (either fighter under 25 % health *(my pick)*), a Beacon about to fall, a Siege Night's last minute | The track's climax section: everything held back is spent here. |

**The four kinds of Fight:**

| Kind | From | Track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skirmish | `CombatReasons.Hostile` (and Blow / Attack against Hostiles) | The place's two fight tracks, 140 to 150 |
| Callout | `PlayerCombatState.InCallout` | The Callout track, 150 to 160 |
| War | `CombatReasons.Held` (ground that is always a fight: wars, The Scar) | The war theme, 165 to 174 |
| Boss | A boss fight (the owner's own system, not built) | The Run's boss track |

**Rules:**

- **Changes land on the next bar of the `MusicConductor` clock.** Fight music is always on the conductor's shared clock
  (both sides of a Callout hear the same beat); Explore beds play on the player's own clock. Going into Alert waits for
  the conductor's next bar, with a stinger over the seam.
- **Stems are drums, bass, harmony and lead,** the same four for every fight track, of the same length, starting
  together, looping on a bar.
- **Numbers go in data:** the Alert distance, the fade times, the lead's trigger, the Peak health share. In `ZoneMusic`
  where they belong to a place, or in one music settings asset for the rest. Never in code.
- **Audio is Presentation:** it reads Gameplay and World (combat state, the zone's rule, faction hostility) and nothing
  in Gameplay ever reads Audio. Cadence already reads the beat only through `IBeatClock` (an interface in Combat that
  Audio fills); the music states keep that shape.
- **What has to reach the player's own game.** Today a client is told only in or out of a fight, and whether it is in a
  Callout; *why* (`PlayerCombatState.Reasons`) is known only where the fight is decided. To tell Alert from Fight and a
  Skirmish from a War, the reasons need to reach the player's game: one small replicated value in Combat, read by
  Audio.
- **Concord keeps its rule.** The town's `ZoneMusic` is `calloutsOnly`: a Hostile fight there stays silent. Skirmish
  music in Concord's Hostile Zones waits for the owner to lift that.

**The hooks that already exist** (`Audio/Runtime`, `Audio/Data`, `Gameplay/Combat/Runtime`):

- `PlayerCombatState.Changed` (in or out of a fight) and `PlayerCombatState.InFightFor(calloutsOnly)`: the fight
  music's only trigger today.
- `CombatMusicPlayer`: books the fight track on the audio clock so it starts on the sample the shared clock asks for.
- `MusicTrack` (a clip, its tempo, first beat and beats to the bar) and `ZoneMusic` (a zone's fight playlist, its gap
  of 2 s, fades of 0.6 s in and 2.5 s out, and `calloutsOnly`), made by **Krytical Nova ▸ Setup ▸ Music**.
- `MusicConductor`: the zone's shared beat; `CombatBeat` and `BeatMath` read it.
- The `KN_Main` mixer: Master, with the groups **Music**, **Sfx**, **UI** and **Ambience**.

The twelve fight tracks the owner picked for Cadence stay as they are until KN-Originals replace them, place by place.

## 5. Ambience plan

A place's sound out of the music is four layers on the **Ambience** group:

1. **A base bed for the zone:** one loop, chosen from the Atlas feel line.
2. **The weather layer**, which exists: `WeatherAmbience` (World) fades its rain and wind loops with the weather.
3. **Day and night:** the bed has a night version, or a night layer over it (crickets, colder wind, quieter traffic).
4. **Point emitters:** 3D sounds on the things that make them, heard as the player walks past: soul pylons (a hum
   that rises close up), harvesters, gantries and drills, ossuaries on chains, waterfalls, forges.

Home Cities add **walla**: a crowd's murmur with no words you can pick out. Terminus has none: its walla is footsteps in
step and the conveyor's clatter, because nobody there raises their voice.

## 7. How tracks are made and approved

1. **Sketch** with ACE-Step 1.5 XL on this PC: several takes per cue from a written brief (coat, tempo, key, the riff,
   instrumental only).
2. **The owner auditions** the sketches on an audition page and picks, by ear. Nothing is used that the owner has not
   heard.
3. **Final** with Google Flow Music, driven through Claude in Chrome, from the picked sketch, **with stems** (drums,
   bass, harmony, lead) for every fight track.
4. **Bake:** `music_bake.py` (the sound-scout skill) levels and cuts the files into the game, `tempo.py` measures the
   tempo and first beat, and **Krytical Nova ▸ Setup ▸ Music** makes the `MusicTrack` and `ZoneMusic` assets. The click
   check (the audition page, `gm.music.click`) has to sit on the beat for the whole loop before a fight track is used.
5. **Into the game** only after the owner approves the baked version.

**Every file gets a provenance line in `cues.json`:** the cue, the tool and model, the prompt, the seed, the date, the
take picked, who approved it and when, the tool's terms as read on the day, and the path in KN-Originals. All of it in
`G:\My Drive\KryticalNova\Audio\Music\KN-Originals`; the repo holds only what is baked.
